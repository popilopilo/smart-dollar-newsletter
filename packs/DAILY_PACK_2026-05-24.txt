--- COPY ---
Here's the uncomfortable math most Swiss banks won't highlight in their glossy brochures.

With current Swiss inflation hovering around 1.1% and savings accounts offering a measly 0.5-0.75% interest, you're experiencing what I call 'silent wealth erosion.' That CHF 50,000 emergency fund you're responsibly maintaining? It's losing approximately CHF 175-300 in real purchasing power every single year.

After 15 years in Switzerland, I've watched countless expats and locals alike fall into this trap—assuming Swiss stability equals Swiss growth. It doesn't.

Your options for 2026:

**Medium-term bonds** – Swiss government bonds now offer slightly better yields than 2023-2024 lows.

**Pillar 3a optimization** – Still the most tax-efficient vehicle for most residents. Max it out before considering taxable investments.

**Diversified ETF portfolios** – Even conservative Swiss investors are increasingly allocating to global index funds.

**High-yield savings accounts** – Some digital banks offer marginally better rates (0.9-1.0%), though still below inflation.

The key isn't abandoning cash entirely—liquidity matters. It's understanding that anything beyond 6 months of expenses sitting in a standard savings account is actively working against you.
