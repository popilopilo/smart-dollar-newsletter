--- COPY ---
Living in Switzerland for over a decade, I can tell you this is genuinely exciting news for savers.

With inflation dropping to 0.8% in May—our lowest since 2021—something remarkable has happened: your savings account is actually growing in real terms. This hasn't been the case for years.

Here's the practical reality on the ground. Most Swiss banks currently offer savings rates between 1.0% and 1.5%. When you subtract that 0.8% inflation, you're looking at positive real returns of 0.2% to 0.7%. Not spectacular, but after years of watching purchasing power erode, it feels like a win.

The SNB holding rates at 1.25% suggests this environment should persist for a while. My advice? If you've been parking money in a 0% current account (and many of us do out of habit), now's the time to move it into a proper savings account or even a medium-term note.

For those with larger sums, consider staggering across different banks to maximize the FINMA deposit protection of CHF 100,000 per institution.

Switzerland continues to be a remarkable outlier in Europe—stable currency, controlled inflation, and now actual returns on savings.
