--- COPY ---
Having lived in Switzerland for 15 years and watched currency cycles closely, here's my take:

The EUR/CHF rate at 0.93 reflects ongoing economic uncertainty in the eurozone and Switzerland's safe-haven status. For those considering franc investments in 2026, understand what you're really buying into.

**The reality check:**
- Swiss francs offer stability, not growth. You're essentially paying a premium for security.
- SNB intervention remains unpredictable. They've historically stepped in when the franc strengthens too rapidly.
- Real returns on CHF deposits are minimal—often negative after accounting for Swiss inflation.

**What I tell my cross-border colleagues:**
If you're earning in euros, you've already lost 7% purchasing power year-over-year. Converting to francs now locks in that loss. However, if you expect further euro weakness, gradual conversion makes sense.

**Practical approach for 2026:**
Consider CHF allocation as insurance rather than investment. A 20-30% portfolio allocation provides hedging without overexposure to a currency that yields almost nothing.

Don't chase the franc because it's "strong." Strong currencies don't automatically mean strong returns. They mean preserved value—which, honestly, is sometimes exactly what you need.
