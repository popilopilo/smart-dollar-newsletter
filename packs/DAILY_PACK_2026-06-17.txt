**What can we expect from SNB interest rates in 2026?**

As someone who's been tracking Swiss monetary policy for years, here's my take on where we're heading.

The SNB's decision to hold rates at 0.25% tells us something important: they're comfortable with the current inflation trajectory around 1.1%. This is classic Swiss central banking—cautious, measured, and focused on long-term stability rather than dramatic moves.

For 2026, I expect rates to remain in the 0-0.50% range unless we see significant external shocks. The SNB has limited room to cut further without returning to negative territory, which they clearly want to avoid after the 2015-2022 experiment.

What does this mean practically?

- **Savers**: Don't expect miracles. That 0.75-1% on CHF savings accounts is likely your ceiling for now.
- **Mortgage holders**: Variable rates should stay favorable, but don't count on significant drops.
- **The franc**: Expect continued strength, which the SNB tolerates as long as inflation stays contained.

The Swiss approach prioritizes stability over stimulus. Boring? Perhaps. But that predictability is exactly why many of us appreciate banking here.
