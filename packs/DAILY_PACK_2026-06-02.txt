Having lived in Switzerland for over a decade and managed CHF-denominated assets, I can offer some perspective on this.

The franc's 4.2% gain against the euro this year reflects exactly what we Swiss residents have come to expect – our currency strengthening during global uncertainty. It's both a blessing and a curse, depending on which side of the border you're shopping.

For 2026 positioning, here's my practical take:

**The SNB factor is real.** The Swiss National Bank has historically intervened when the franc gets "too strong" – it hurts our export-heavy economy. They've been quieter lately, but don't assume this continues indefinitely.

**If you're holding CHF:** Your purchasing power in the eurozone is genuinely impressive right now. Travel, property hunting in France or Italy, euro-denominated investments – all effectively discounted.

**If you're considering CHF exposure:** The safe-haven premium is already priced in. You're not getting a bargain entry point.

My honest advice? The franc rarely disappoints long-term holders, but expecting another 4%+ gain assumes continued global instability and SNB passivity. Neither is guaranteed.
