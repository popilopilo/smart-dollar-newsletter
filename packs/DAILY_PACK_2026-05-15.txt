Great question, and one that hits close to home for many of us living near the Swiss border.

With EUR/CHF currently hovering at 0.93, cross-border workers from France and Germany are seeing their purchasing power squeezed by 4.2% compared to January. If you're paid in euros but spend in francs, consider these practical strategies:

**Timing your conversions** matters enormously. Rather than converting your entire salary immediately, use a multi-currency account (Wise, Revolut, or traditional Swiss banks offer these) and convert in tranches when rates dip favorably.

**Negotiate currency clauses** if possible. Some employers near the border now offer partial CHF payments or exchange rate adjustments—it never hurts to ask.

**Shift spending strategically.** Stock up on groceries and fuel on the French/German side. The price differential often exceeds the exchange rate loss, especially for fuel where you can save 30-40 centimes per liter.

The SNB has shown little appetite for intervention recently, and with Swiss inflation remaining stubbornly low compared to the Eurozone, expect the franc to stay strong through Q3 at minimum.
