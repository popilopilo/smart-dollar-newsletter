--- COPY ---
The Swiss interest rate environment in 2026 is looking increasingly challenging for savers. With the SNB policy rate currently at 0.25% and another cut widely anticipated on June 19th, we're heading toward near-zero or potentially negative territory again.

As someone managing finances here in Switzerland, I've been watching savings account yields evaporate in real-time. What was 1.5% a year ago is now often 0.5% or less at major banks like UBS and PostFinance.

Medium-term bonds and fixed-income ETFs still offer 1.2-1.8% yields, but here's the catch – these rates won't last. If you want to lock in current yields, you need to act before the June cut. Once the SNB moves, bond prices will adjust and new issuances will reflect lower rates.

Practical options worth considering:
- Kassenobligationen (medium-term notes) with 3-5 year terms
- Swiss government bond ETFs for liquidity
- Pillar 3a accounts, which still offer slightly better rates due to tax advantages

The frank remains strong, which limits inflation concerns, but yield-seeking Swiss investors may need to look beyond traditional savings vehicles or accept that capital preservation, not growth, is the realistic goal.
