--- COPY ---
The Swiss National Bank (SNB) has maintained its policy rate at 0.25% as of its most recent decision, adopting a cautious stance given the current global economic uncertainty.

For 2026 projections, most analysts expect the SNB to keep rates in the 0-0.5% range, though this heavily depends on inflation trends and what the ECB and Fed do. Switzerland's inflation remains remarkably contained compared to neighboring countries, giving the SNB flexibility.

What does this mean practically? Swiss savings accounts at neobanks like Yuh, Neon, or Radicant are currently offering 0.8-1.2% interest—not exciting, but remember we spent years in negative territory. That's actually positive real return given Swiss inflation hovering around 1%.

For those of us living here, the key takeaway: don't expect dramatic rate movements either direction. The SNB prioritizes stability and currency management over aggressive monetary policy shifts.

My recommendation? If you're holding significant CHF cash, consider splitting between a high-yield savings account and perhaps Säule 3a contributions where tax benefits often outweigh pure interest considerations.
