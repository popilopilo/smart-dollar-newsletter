Having lived through the negative interest rate era in Switzerland, I can tell you this feels eerily familiar.

The SNB is almost certainly cutting rates tomorrow—markets are pricing in an 80% probability of a 25 basis point reduction, which would bring our policy rate down to just 0.25%. For those of us with Swiss bank accounts, this is painful news.

The average savings account here already pays a measly 0.8%. Once this cut filters through, expect those rates to compress even further. Some banks may start offering effectively nothing again.

What's driving this? The usual suspects: the franc remains stubbornly strong, inflation is well below target, and the SNB is trying to stay ahead of the ECB's dovish trajectory. Chairman Schlegel has made it clear that protecting Swiss exporters from currency appreciation remains a priority.

For Swiss residents, the practical implications are clear: keeping large cash balances in CHF savings accounts is becoming increasingly costly in real terms. Many of us are looking at money market funds, short-duration bonds, or simply accepting that cash is for liquidity, not returns.

The 2020s have not been kind to Swiss savers.
