Having lived in Switzerland for over a decade and watched SNB policy closely, here's my take on where rates are heading.

The SNB has been notably dovish compared to the ECB and Fed. After cutting rates multiple times in 2024, we're currently sitting at historically low levels. Looking toward 2026, most analysts expect the SNB to maintain accommodative policy, potentially holding rates between 0.25-0.75% depending on inflation trajectory and franc strength.

The critical factor? The franc's persistent appreciation. With EUR/CHF around 0.94 and USD/CHF at 0.89, the SNB faces constant pressure. A strong franc keeps imported inflation low but squeezes our export-dependent economy.

My educated guess: the SNB will likely keep rates near the lower bound through 2026, possibly even flirting with negative territory again if the franc surges further. They've shown they're not afraid to diverge from other central banks when protecting Swiss economic interests.

For practical planning—whether you're investing, taking out a mortgage, or just timing currency exchanges for travel—expect continued low rates but prepare for volatility. The SNB has surprised us before.
